Straight Answer

Why We Will Not Put Two Prices Next to Each Other

You came here wanting a number against a number. We can only give you one of them, and it is worth thirty seconds to explain why that is honesty rather than a dodge.

The can has a published rate because it is the same object on every job. A 14-yard roll-off going to a remodel in Pearland is the identical piece of steel going to a garage in League City. Nothing about your project changes what the container costs, so we print the number: on the 14-yard it is $365 for a day, $365 for the weekend, $399 for three days and $525 for seven. On the 20-yard it is $500, $550 and $650 for one, three and seven days. Any day past that is $100.

Junk removal has no list price because you are not renting an object. You are buying volume in our trailer, hands to move it, and a trip to a facility that charges us by what is on board. Two garages of the same square footage can be completely different jobs. One is empty moving boxes and a bike. The other is paint cans, a chest freezer, a weight bench, and forty years of settled clutter. Charging both the same would mean overcharging one of you.

So a junk removal number given before anyone has looked is either padded to cover the worst case, or it gets quietly revised in your driveway. We would rather do neither.

Here is the honest way to compare: text a couple of photos to 832-425-7749, take the free quote we send back, and hold it up against $365. That is two real numbers, and it costs you one text message.

How to Actually Decide

Five Things That Settle It

Run your job through these in order. Most people know their answer by the third one.

Factor OneThe Shape of the Job

Not the size, the shape. Ask yourself whether this is one event or a stretch of time.

A pile that already exists, sitting in the garage waiting for somebody to deal with it, is one event. It is not going to grow. That is a job for a crew and a single visit.

A project that manufactures debris as it goes is a stretch of time. Demo Saturday, drywall the next weekend, trim after that. Calling us out three separate times for a job like that is silly when a container could just sit there absorbing everything. Shape beats volume almost every time.

Factor TwoWho Is Actually Lifting

Be honest with yourself here, because the driveway is a bad place to discover you were optimistic.

A homeowner steadily working through a garage over a weekend, mostly boxes and bags and things one person can carry, is a great fit for a can. You will be sore and you will be finished.

A sleeper sofa is not that. Neither is a treadmill, a gun safe, a full chest freezer, a hot tub, or anything at the top of a staircase. Those are the jobs where people rent a container, get two items in, and call us anyway. If your list has more than a couple of items in that category, skip the middle step.

Age, a bad back, a recent surgery, or simply not having a second pair of hands all count as real answers. Nobody is scoring you.

Factor ThreeWhere a Can Would Even Go

This one rules out the dumpster more often than anything else, and it has nothing to do with your junk.

A roll-off needs a straight shot for the truck, a spot that stays clear for the whole rental, and nothing hanging low overhead. Basketball goals, low limbs, and a car nobody can move are the usual culprits. Shared driveways get complicated fast, since your neighbor did not sign up for a week of this.

The 14-yard is the reason we can say yes on lots where other outfits say no. It is the small end of the roll-off family. The 20 and 30 yard cans most companies lead with are both longer and taller, and on a short suburban driveway that difference decides the whole thing. Worth checking your association rules too, since some have opinions about how many days a container can sit out front.

If the answer is that there is nowhere sensible to put it, that is not a problem to solve. That is your decision made for you, and we will load it instead.

Factor FourWhether You Know the Volume

People are reliably bad at guessing how much is in a room, and that is not an insult. Clutter compresses in your memory.

The problem is that guessing wrong on a dumpster costs money in both directions. Overfill it and you are booking a second one. Half fill it and you paid for air. Neither is a disaster, but both are avoidable.

If you genuinely cannot picture the volume, a quote visit is the cheapest thing on this page. It is free, it takes a few minutes, and you walk away with a real number and a straight opinion on whether one can would have covered it. Sometimes we look at a garage and tell you to rent the can. That is a normal outcome for us.

Factor FiveWhat the Material Is

Some things are simply not allowed in a container, and finding out after you have loaded it is a bad afternoon.

The short version: Freon appliances, televisions and monitors, tires, and batteries stay out of the can, though we can take most of them for you on the junk removal side. Chemicals, wet paint, fuel, and propane are off limits either way and need a proper drop-off.

Heavy material like tile, mortar, brick, or shingles is fine in the can, we just want a heads up first so we can talk through how to load it safely. Brush and green waste are the opposite problem: light, but so bulky it eats container space in a hurry.

Rather than repeat the whole list here, we keep it in one place. Read what cannot go in the dumpster before you book either service.

Worked Examples

Four Jobs We Get Called About Constantly

Find the one closest to yours. The reasoning matters more than the verdict, because your version will be a little different.

Usually both, quote first

Cleaning Out a Parent's House

Three things are true at once here and they pull in different directions. Nobody knows the volume, including the family. The sorting is slow because half of it is not junk, it is decisions. And the furniture is heavy in a house that was never designed to be emptied.

What works most often is a split. A can in the driveway gives everyone weeks to go room by room without a crew standing there watching. Then we come at the end for the beds, the appliances, the console furniture, and whatever is still standing after the family has taken what they want.

But if the house has to be listed on a specific date, be realistic and book the crew for the whole thing. The slow sort is a lovely plan that dies against a closing date, and paying for a container that sits half full for a week while everybody is busy is the worst of both.

Rent the can, take the 7

A Kitchen Remodel Across Two Weekends

Classic dumpster job, and it is the shape that decides it. Cabinets come out Saturday. Tile and backer board the next day. Then drywall scrap, packaging, and the old range trailing behind over the following week. Nothing about that wants a crew showing up three separate times.

On the tier, do the arithmetic instead of guessing. On the 14-yard, three days at $399 plus one extra day is $499, so a genuine four-day job is cheaper booked that way than as a seven, and once you are at five days or more the flat $525 is the better buy. On the 20-yard the maths flips: $550 plus one extra day is $650, the same money as the 7-day week, so book the week if you might run long and the last three days come free. Two weekends plus the week between them means you want the seven, comfortably.

One caution: tile, mortar, and cast iron get heavy faster than people expect. Tell us it is going in and we will talk through how to load it so the can hauls safely.

Call us, skip the can

One Heavy Sectional and a Treadmill

This is the clearest no on the page, and it is a no to the cheaper-sounding option. Two items will not come close to filling a 14-yard container. You would be paying to park a mostly empty steel box in your driveway.

Worse, the container does nothing about the actual difficulty. A sectional still has to come through a doorway that was not measured for it, and a treadmill still has to come down the stairs it went up in pieces. That part is the entire job. Renting a can just relocates the hard bit to you.

This is precisely what a two-person crew is for. Snap a photo of both, text it to 832-425-7749, and you will usually have a free quote back without anyone driving out. It is one visit and you never touch either one.

Depends on the truck route

Clearing an Acre of Brush

Here the deciding factor is not the brush, it is where a vehicle can physically get to. Brush is light and enormously bulky, so it fills container volume faster than almost anything else. An acre of it may well be more than one can holds.

If a truck and trailer can reach the pile, the trailer is usually the better tool, because we drive to the brush instead of making you drag the brush across a property to a fixed container. That is the whole advantage and on acreage it is a big one.

If you are cutting over several weekends and there is firm, level ground near the drive where a can can be set and later picked up, the can earns its keep. Soft ground after a wet week is a real consideration out here. Either way, do yourself a favor and rake it into one pile rather than five. One pile loads dramatically faster, and a faster load is an easier day for everybody.

The Third Option

Plenty of Jobs Want Both

This gets treated like a coin flip and it really is not. Some of our best days are half and half, and it usually saves people money rather than costing them more.

You take the volume, we take the weight. The can handles boxes, bags, clothes, paper, and everything you want to go through slowly. We handle the piano, the safe, the fridge with Freon in it, and the shed nobody wants to open. You are not paying a crew to carry pillows, and you are not throwing your back out over an appliance.

The can cannot take all of it anyway. Old televisions, Freon appliances, tires, and batteries have to come out separately no matter how you organize the rest. That alone puts a lot of cleanouts into the both column without anyone planning it that way.

Contractors run this pattern by default. The container lives on site through the build so the crew can keep working, then we come at the end for the awkward leftovers nobody wants to load: old cabinets, a cast iron tub, the pallet of packaging in the garage.

Booking both is one phone call. Tell us both halves up front and we will sequence it properly, so the can is not sitting in your driveway burning rental days while you wait on a crew slot. That coordination is free and it is the part people forget to ask for.

Choosing Between Them

Questions People Ask Before They Book

Which one is cheaper, honestly?

We cannot answer that in the abstract, and anyone who does is guessing. The can has a published number because it is the same object every time: $365 for a day, $399 for three and $525 for seven on the 14-yard, or $500, $550 and $650 on the 20-yard. Junk removal has no list price because you are not buying an object, you are buying volume, labor, and a trip to the facility. So do it in the right order. Text a couple of photos to 832-425-7749, take the free quote we send back, and hold that number against $365. Now you are comparing two real numbers instead of two vibes.

Will a 14-yard dumpster actually fit in my driveway?

On a normal residential driveway, usually yes, and that is exactly why our cans stay on a 14 ft by 7 ft footprint. Both the 14-yard and the 20-yard sit on that same footprint, and the 30 yard cans most companies lead with are longer and taller, and on a short drive or a tight lot that difference is the whole ball game. What we need is a straight approach for the truck, a spot that stays clear for the length of the rental, and nothing low overhead like a basketball goal or a limb. Shared driveways and short drives are worth a phone call before you book. Send a photo of the spot and we will tell you yes or no.

What if I rent the can and then cannot lift half of it?

That happens plenty and it is not a failure. Call us. We will come load the heavy pieces into the can you already have, or haul them off separately on the junk removal side if they are things the can cannot legally take. You do not have to pick one service and then live with it. Plenty of our best jobs are half and half.

How do I know if everything will fit in one dumpster?

Photos get you most of the way there. Send us shots of the room, the garage, or the pile from a few angles and we will tell you straight whether it looks like one can, more than one, or a job better suited to the trailer. If it is genuinely hard to call, we will come look for free. A can you overfill turns into a second can, and a can you half fill is money spent on empty space, so it is worth two minutes on the phone first.

Can I keep the dumpster longer than I booked?

Yes, extra days are $100 each. Worth knowing the math before you pick a tier. On the 14-yard, three days at $399 plus one extra day comes to $499, so a four day project is cheaper booked that way than as a seven, and from five days on the flat $525 is the better buy. On the 20-yard it flips: three days at $550 plus a single extra day is $650, the exact same money as the 7-day, so if you might run past three days, book the week and get the rest of it free. Tell us the real timeline and we will point you at the cheaper option even when it is the smaller one.

Is there anything you can haul that I am not allowed to put in the can?

Yes, and this catches people. Refrigerators and AC units still holding Freon, TVs and monitors, tires, and batteries cannot go in the dumpster, but we can take most of them for you on the junk removal side. Chemicals, paint, fuel, and propane are a no on both. The full restricted list lives on our dumpster rental process page. If you are unsure about one item, text a photo before you toss it in rather than after.
