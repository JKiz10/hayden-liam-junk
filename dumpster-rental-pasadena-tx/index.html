City Dump or Roll-Off

The City Drop-Off Is Free. Here Is Where It Runs Out.

Before you rent anything, know what Pasadena already gives you. As of October 2026, the City of Pasadena's garbage service page says residents can bring one load of household refuse a month, up to 2 cubic yards (about a half-ton pickup load), to the city's landfill drop-off at 550 Old Genoa-Red Bluff Road. The site has a Houston mailing address, ZIP 77034, but it is the city's own.

The catches are real. You need a valid Texas driver's license and a current, original Pasadena water bill with an address that matches it, because the city does not take copies. The load has to stay below the top of a pickup frame and be tarped, and a hard hat and safety vest are required on site. Hours are 7 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Monday through Friday and 7 a.m. to 10:30 a.m. on Saturday. Go past 2 cubic yards and the city's minimum charge starts at $140.00 and varies from there.

Placement

Where the Can Sits in Pasadena

Nearly every roll-off we deliver ends up on a driveway, and Pasadena is no different. The can is 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide and slides off the trailer, so the spot needs flat, hard ground, a straight approach, and nothing overhead. Pace it off before you book, starting at the garage door or carport pad and heading toward the curb, with a few feet to spare.

Tell us about anything that narrows the spot: a drive that slopes toward the street, a meter box near the edge, a low limb, cracked concrete. Photos from the curb help, and we would rather change the spot before the can is down than after.

What about the street? Honestly, Pasadena's public trash pages do not say. We went through the city's garbage service and heavy trash pages in October 2026, and neither mentions where a roll-off may sit: curb, street, or right-of-way. Until the city says otherwise, the driveway is the plan. If yours cannot take a 14-foot can, phone City Hall at 713-477-1511 before booking and pass along the answer, and we will plan the drop around it.

What Fills It

Mid-Century Houses, Fence Lines, and Turnovers

By the Census Bureau's 2024 estimates, Pasadena's median home was built in 1976, and about half the houses in town date from the 1950s through the 1970s. The city grew fast in that stretch, from about 22,000 people in 1950 to nearly 90,000 by 1970, according to the Texas State Historical Association. Houses that old have usually been through a roof or two and a few fences, and that is what ends up in our cans.

Fences. A rotted run of pickets, posts, and an old gate makes a bigger pile than it looks like on the lawn. Pasadena's fence ordinance exempts a replacement that keeps the same material and the same location from needing a permit, but changing either one does need a permit, and a fence cannot go within 5 feet of an open ditch, swale, or bayou. Read it before the old fence comes down.

Interiors. Carpet and pad come up in long rolls, then tile, subfloor, a vanity, a tub surround. Cabinets from a kitchen refresh are bulky even when they are light. These jobs run for days, and a can in the driveway lets you toss as you go.

Rental turnovers. About 46% of Pasadena households rent, so landlords and property managers watch doors flip all year. Mattresses, bagged clothes, broken blinds, and tired carpet make a quick can. If you would rather not carry any of it, our Pasadena junk removal crew loads it for you.

Sheds and storms. A backyard shed that has leaned for ten years comes down in panels, and whatever was inside comes with it. Storm season adds limbs, fence panels, and wet drywall.

Curbside Pickup

What Pasadena Heavy Trash Will and Will Not Take

Pasadena households are served by more than one provider, and the two the city's pages cover for bulk pickup are city crews and Frontier Waste Solutions. Each has its own schedule. City Sanitation at 713-475-7884 can tell you which is yours.

For households on city crews, the heavy trash page puts pickup on Wednesdays. If your garbage days are Monday and Thursday, small piles go in the first and third weeks of the month, and if they are Tuesday and Friday, in the second and fourth. A pile too big for a standard garbage truck should be out by 7 a.m. on the Monday of your week, and crews collect those through the week.

On Frontier streets, bulk and brush pickup comes every other week on an assigned week, set out before 7 a.m. Frontier takes small furniture, mattresses, rugs, TVs, and fence material. It does not take tires, electronics, appliances, household hazardous waste, or debris from commercial contractors and service providers. Appliances and tires go through city Sanitation by phone.

Read that list as a guide to when a can pays off. Curbside bulk is built for the odd couch, not a gutted room, and a remodel, a contractor's tear-out, or a whole fence line is more than either service is set up to collect.

Read the Paperwork

Deed Restrictions and the Clock

Pasadena does not have one rulebook for every block. If your street has deed restrictions, the city's planning page describes them as instruments that run with the land. City staff are not authorized to interpret them, and the city does not keep copies. The Harris County Clerk does. Find yours before the can arrives, because a letter about a container in public view lands on you, not on us.

Then tell us your window. If a restriction rules out any container at all, our crew loads straight onto the trailer and drives away in the same visit, so nothing sits out overnight. That is junk removal in Pasadena.

Can I use the city drop-off on Old Genoa-Red Bluff Road instead of renting a can?

Can I put a dumpster in the street in front of my Pasadena house?

Pasadena's public garbage and heavy trash pages say nothing about parking a roll-off in the street or on the right-of-way, and we will not make up a rule. Our cans go on driveways. Will Pasadena's heavy trash pickup take my remodel debris?
