Placement First

Driveway, Street, or Side Yard?

Price is the first question. Where the can sits decides whether the week goes smoothly, and Seabrook's city code has real language about the front of a lot.

We read the city's code of ordinances and its permit list. We found no permit for a rented dumpster and no section that names one. The right-of-way permit in Section 75-46 covers digging, cutting, excavating, and drilling in the right-of-way, and a container on pavement is none of those. Other sections still matter: the code bars putting trash on a street, and a parked vehicle has to leave at least 10 feet of the roadway open. So the street is the spot we steer people away from.

The city's permit page also says Todville, Red Bluff, and Repsdorph Roads are Harris County right-of-way, so on those the street question belongs to the county.

A paved driveway is where we set almost every can. The city's front-yard parking rule names concrete, brick, pavers, and asphalt as acceptable surfaces, and we put boards under the can either way. A lawn is a different conversation: a loaded can is heavy and soft ground gives. If grass is the only open spot, text us a photo before you book.

Have a side-yard spot a truck can reach? Tell us. The code measures the front yard back from the front corners of the house, so a can beside or behind the house is not sitting in it.

The Seabrook Rule to Know

Seventy-Two Hours in a Front Yard

Seabrook's zoning code has an outside-storage section for each residential district. In the single-family R-1 district it is Section 3.03.08, and the other residential districts match. Outside storage means an item placed outside for more than 72 hours in a 12-month period. The code then lists what may not sit in a front yard past that point, starting with debris, rubbish, and trash, then new, used, or discarded building materials.

A rented roll-off is not named anywhere in that section. Whether the city reads a loaded can in a front yard as stored debris is the city's call, not ours, and we will not pretend to settle it here.

Two exemptions sound close. Trash and bulky waste waiting for pickup by the city or another sanitation service is exempt, but only in a container the city approves, set behind the front yard. Building materials kept in a workmanlike way for an active building permit are exempt until two weeks after the work ends. Neither mentions a rented can, so ask before you lean on either.

Here is how we plan around the clock. A one-day rental and the $399.99 weekend special, Saturday morning to Sunday night, both finish well inside 72 hours. A three-day rental sits right at the edge. A seven-day rental is well past it, so for that length we want the can beside or behind the house, or an answer from the city, before we deliver.

The city's Community Development office handles building permits and zoning questions, and its number, (281) 291-5669, is on the city's Building page. Ask whether a rented roll-off in a residential front yard counts as outside storage, and whether an active building permit changes the answer. Codes change, so check the links on this page before you rely on them.

Load Plan

Bayside Houses, Bayside Projects

Seabrook sits on Galveston Bay, and the loads follow the water. The city's flood ordinance adds 18 inches of freeboard above base flood elevation and counts any repair or improvement costing 50 percent or more of a building's market value as a substantial improvement. If a gut-and-rebuild is on your list, talk to the city's Building Official first.

Roofs. New roofs and roof repairs are on the city's permit list, and roofing is one of the things the city's curbside bulk pickup refuses. A tear-off is a can job. We do not charge by the ton, but heavy loads still have a safe towing limit, so tell us how big the roof is before you book.

Docks, piers, and bulkheads. The city requires permits for bulkheads, piers, docks, boat houses, and riprap, new work and repairs. The old decking and boards go in the can. Riprap, rock, and concrete are dense, so call before you load them. Sort the permit first and the demolition second.

Remodels and construction sites. Siding, windows, doors, patio covers, and driveway or patio replacement are on the permit list too. If you are building or demolishing, Seabrook's construction-site rules ask owners and contractors to keep scrap in containers, haul it off at least every other week, and keep the street and the neighbors' property clear of debris. A rented can is the plain way to meet that, and how it squares with the front-yard clock on your lot is a question for the city.

Tires, batteries, wet paint, chemicals, and appliances that still hold refrigerant stay out of any can. The process page has our full list.

City Pickup

Bulk Day Covers Less Than You Think

The city's trash and recycling come from Waste Management of Texas under a city contract. Which side of State Highway 146 you live on sets your day: west of 146 is the Green Zone and east is the Yellow Zone, with bulk collection every other Monday in one and every other Tuesday in the other. The city's trash page has the current details.

Bulk pickup is limited to two items per collection, with nothing over 200 pounds. Boxes must be broken down and carpet cut into 4-foot bundles. The city refuses concrete, brick, asphalt, roofing, contractor construction debris, tires, paint, batteries, and motor oil.

More than two bulky items is already past the limit, and a roof, a torn-out deck, or a bathroom gutted to the studs is more than bulk day will touch, whatever the count.

Roll-Off FAQ

Seabrook Dumpster Questions

Can I put a dumpster in the street in Seabrook?

We found no permit for it and no rule naming a rented dumpster, but that does not make the street a good idea. The code bars trash on a street, a parked vehicle must leave 10 feet of roadway open, and Todville, Red Bluff, and Repsdorph Roads are Harris County right-of-way. We recommend the driveway or a side yard. If your job truly needs the street, call the city's Community Development office at (281) 291-5669 first and tell us what they say.

How long can a rented dumpster sit in a Seabrook front yard?

The city does not name rented dumpsters, so we cannot give you a number it has signed off on. Residential zoning limits debris, trash, and discarded building materials in a front yard to 72 hours in a 12-month period, and the exemptions may or may not reach a rented can. A one-day rental at $379.99 or the $399.99 weekend special fits. For longer, ask the city or set the can beside or behind the house.

What won't Seabrook's bulk pickup take?

Bulk pickup runs every other week, takes two items, and nothing over 200 pounds. It refuses concrete, brick, asphalt, roofing, contractor construction debris, tires, paint, batteries, and motor oil. Your day depends on which side of State Highway 146 you live on, and the city trash page has the details. More than two items, roofing, or demolition means a can makes sense.

Which rental length should I book for a Seabrook job?

On the 14-yard, one day is $379.99, the weekend special is $399.99, three days is $419.99, and seven days is $549.99. On the 20-yard, one day is $499.99, three days is $549.99, and seven days is $649.99, with no weekend rate. Delivery and haul-off are included. An extra day is $50 if the can is available.

Does the 20-yard need more driveway than the 14-yard?

No. Both cans are 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide. The 20-yard's sides are taller, about 6 feet, so you can choose by how much you are throwing away instead of how much driveway you have. Neither has a weight or tonnage fee. Fill it to the rim and keep it level.

Can I load old dock boards, decking, or bulkhead wood?

Boards, decking, and railings go in like any other lumber, loaded level with the rim. Rock, riprap, and concrete are dense, so call before you load them. If you are not sure what a piece of wood has been treated with, text us a photo first. Dock, pier, bulkhead, and boat house work needs a city permit, so sort that out before demolition starts.
