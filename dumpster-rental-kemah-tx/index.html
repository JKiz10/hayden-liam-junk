Placement First

Where the Can Sits in a Town This Small

Kemah covers a bit under two square miles on the western shore of Galveston Bay. Seabrook sits across the Clear Creek Channel to the north, League City wraps around the west and south, and State Highway 146 runs through the middle. Lots are modest and a good share of the houses stand on columns, so settle where a steel box 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide is going to sit before you pick a size.

Our answer is almost always your driveway. A roll-off needs flat, hard ground, a straight run in from the street and nothing hanging low over the spot. At a raised house that means the drive beside the stairs, not the space underneath. Text a photo of the approach to 832-425-7749 and we will tell you whether the trailer can back in.

The street is where we stop and point you to City Hall. We read through Kemah's code of ordinances on Municode and found nothing that says whether a roll-off may sit in the street or right-of-way, and no permit set up for one. What we did find is Section 38-1, titled Dumpsters. It says a refuse bin or dumpster kept on a site must be enclosed by a latched, secure fence. That reads like a rule for permanent bins behind businesses and apartment buildings, and nothing on the city's website explains how it applies to a temporary can on a driveway. We are not going to guess for you. The city's permits page lists City Hall at 1401 State Highway 146 and 281-334-1611, so ask there. Until the city tells you something different, the driveway is the plan.

One more layer: deed restrictions. Section 18-3 says Kemah enforces recorded ones, so if your street has them, read the parts about containers and debris.

Before You Book

Where City Pickup Runs Out

Garbage service in Kemah belongs to AmeriWaste, the city's contractor. The city's Trash and Recycling page sets out the routine: carts at the curb by 7 a.m. for Monday and Thursday garbage, recycling on Tuesday, and Thursday as the day for heavy trash.

  • Two items per pickup. Furniture, appliances and mattresses cannot be over 50 pounds, and the page says additional fees may apply.
  • AmeriWaste crews do not go onto private property, so everything has to be out at the curb.
  • Appliances with refrigerant must be drained and tagged by a certified recycler, and water heaters drained.
  • Brush and green waste is capped at six cans, bags or bundles a week, with bundles no longer than five feet, 18 inches across and 50 pounds.
  • Sheetrock, concrete, lumber and bricks count as construction debris. That is a separate call to AmeriWaste at 281-331-8400, and fees may apply.

Read that list as a guide to when a can makes sense. A worn-out recliner or two? Curb it. A kitchen, a floor or a fence is more than two items, heavier than 50 pounds a piece and made of the materials the city does not treat as ordinary trash. The city's service flyer adds that Kemah itself will not run on-call debris pickups. A rented can fills that gap and lets you load across a few days instead of one morning.

The Loads

What Actually Fills a Can in Kemah

The work follows the housing and the water. Kemah has old bay houses (the one in FEMA's story dates to about 1919), houses raised on columns, and streets with names like Marina Bay Drive and Harborside Way.

Flood and storm gut-outs. Kemah's elevation is listed at about 6.6 feet. Hurricane Ike came ashore on September 13, 2008. In the FEMA account the surge reached about 12 feet at that house, and many neighboring homes were destroyed. After Harvey in 2017, the city's community development director told Community Impact that about 50 structures in Kemah took on water. When water gets in, the can fills with wet drywall, insulation, flooring, cabinets and soaked furniture, and speed matters.

Before you demo anything big, ask about the 50 percent line. Kemah's flood chapter defines substantial damage and substantial improvement as damage or work that costs half or more of the building's market value, and in mapped flood zones those projects have to put the lowest floor 18 inches above the base flood level. That is a call to the Building Department at 281-334-1611, and it is better to make it before the drywall is in the can.

Roof tear-offs. TWIA's windstorm certification page lists replacing a roof, or a significant part of one, among the improvements that need a certificate of compliance for coastal property owners, so ask your roofer who is handling that paperwork. Tell us the roof size and the number of layers and we will help you pick the can. Shingles are dense, so keep the load level with the rim.

Raising or rebuilding the lower level. Many Kemah houses are already raised, and the same Community Impact story says new homes and commercial buildings must sit at least 1.5 feet above base flood elevation, with many owners building higher to leave room to park underneath. In the FEMA account, the surge took the steps, a dock and the breakaway walls from under that house. Clearing or rebuilding a lower level means hauling old lattice, wall panels, framing and stored odds and ends. It runs for weeks, which is where the 7-day rate earns its keep.

Fences, decks and sheds. Pickets, posts, deck boards and shed walls are bulky, and a fence line is more volume than it looks from the yard. Cut long panels down so they lie flat.

Floors, kitchens and baths. Carpet and pad, tile, cabinets and old fixtures make a dense mix. There is no weight fee, so load level with the rim and tell us first if the load is mostly tile, concrete or dirt.

Local Rules

The Kemah Rules That Touch a Rented Can

Covered, clean and not overflowing. In the part of the code about keeping pollution out of storm drains (Section 38-265), anyone renting a dumpster in Kemah is told to keep it covered at all times, keep the can and the ground around it clean, and rent a size big enough that trash does not spill over. We tarp every load before it leaves, and if your pile looks bigger than the can, take the 20-yard.

Job sites. If a contractor is running your project, Section 18-79 makes the property owner and the contractor responsible for keeping the street, public property and neighbors' property clear of debris and silt. It also says the site is policed daily, garbage leaves at least twice a week and other scrap at least every other week. Ask your contractor how they plan to meet that.

Permits belong to the project. We found no permit for the can itself on private property. The work often needs one. The city's permit FAQ says most construction, remodeling and repair projects need a permit and to check with the Building Department before starting.

Questions From Kemah Customers

Do I need a permit to put a roll-off on my Kemah driveway?

We found no permit for a dumpster on private property in the Kemah code of ordinances. We did find Section 38-1, which says a refuse bin or dumpster kept on a site has to be enclosed by a latched, secure fence. It looks aimed at permanent bins, and the city's website does not say how it applies to a temporary can, so call City Hall at 281-334-1611 before you book. The work itself may need a permit, which is a separate question.

Can the dumpster go in the street in front of my house?

We cannot tell you yes. We found nothing in the Kemah code that says a roll-off may sit in the street or right-of-way, and no permit set up for one. Our advice is to use the driveway. If the driveway truly will not work, ask the city first, then tell us what they said and we will plan the drop around it.

What will the city's Thursday heavy trash pickup not take?

AmeriWaste, the city's trash contractor, takes two heavy items on Thursday, each under 50 pounds, and only from the curb. Sheetrock, concrete, lumber and bricks count as construction debris and need a separate call to 281-331-8400, and fees may apply. Anything bigger than that is the kind of job a roll-off is for.

How long should I rent the can for a flood gut-out or a rebuild?

For one wet room, the $379.99 one-day 14-yard or the $399.99 weekend special is usually enough if your helpers are ready on day one. A kitchen, a floor or a roof is more comfortable on the 3-day rate, $419.99 for the 14-yard. If you are rebuilding the lower level of a raised house or waiting on inspections, take the 7-day at $549.99. An extra day is $50 if the can is available.

Does the dumpster have to be covered in Kemah?

The code says yes. Anyone renting a dumpster in Kemah has to keep it covered at all times, keep the area around it clean, and rent a size big enough that it does not overflow. We tarp every load before it leaves. Ask us how to keep it covered between loading days, and keep the pile level with the rim.

Is the 20-yard worth it for a Kemah gut job?

Often, yes. The 20-yard is $499.99 for a day, $549.99 for three days and $649.99 for a week, against the 14-yard's $379.99, $419.99 and $549.99.
