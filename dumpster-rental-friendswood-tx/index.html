Start Here

Driveway First, Street Last

Almost every can we set goes on a driveway, and in Friendswood that is the plan we would give you even if the street were wide open. Single-family detached houses make up about 83 percent of the city's housing, going by the Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates, and plenty of those lots have a drive with room for a can that is 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide. Walk yours with a tape before you book.

The street is a different story, and the plain answer is that Friendswood does not publish a simple rule for it. Before writing this page we went through the city's permit pages and its code of ordinances on Municode, looking for the words dumpster, roll-off, and container. We did not find a dumpster permit, a fee, or a form. What the code does say is narrower. Section 70-20 requires a permit from the city manager, with notice to the police chief, before building or remodeling work blocks a paved street or alley. Section 26-106 makes it unlawful to leave rubbish on the sidewalk or in the street in front of your house. And the city's code enforcement office lists right-of-way complaints among the things it investigates. None of those sections names a dumpster, so we will not tell you a street drop is banned and we will not tell you it is fine.

So plan on the driveway. If it truly will not work, say because of a steep apron or a shared drive, call Community Development at 281-996-3201 before you book and ask the question out loud. The number comes from the city's own inspections and permits page.

On delivery day we need two things from you: a clear driveway and clear air above it. Move the cars, then look up. A 14-foot can needs headroom as it comes off the trailer, and a low limb is the usual snag. Section 70-81 of the city code already tells owners to keep limbs at least 15 feet above the street and 8 feet above the strip between the pavement and the property line, so the street side is normally fine. The stretch from the curb to your garage door is yours to check. Unsure? Text us a photo of the approach.

What Fills Cans Here

Houses From 1970 to 2009, and What They Need Next

The Census Bureau counts about 15,500 homes in Friendswood, with a median build year of 1991. Roughly four out of five went up between 1970 and 2009, and the biggest single group, around 3,500 homes, dates from the 1970s. Older streets such as Sunmeadow, Canterbury Park, and Imperial Gardens come from that decade. A house that age is due for a roof, a floor, or a fence, and each of those ends up in a can.

  • Roof tear-offs. Shingles are the heaviest thing most people ever put in a can. There is no weight fee, but shingles still reach a safe towing weight before the can looks full, so call before you start loading and we will tell you where to stop.
  • Fences and sheds. A worn privacy fence is long, splintered, and bulkier than it looks. The city's permit page lists fences and storage buildings among the work that needs a permit, so check what yours requires before the old one comes down.
  • Kitchens, baths, and floors. Cabinet runs, tile, underlayment, carpet and pad. One bathroom fits the smaller can. A kitchen plus the flooring through the rest of the house wants the bigger one.
  • Driveway and patio concrete. Broken slab is dense. Call first and we will tell you how far to fill.
  • Garages and move-outs. Nearly one Friendswood household in four rents, and every move-out leaves a pile behind.

Storm cleanup is its own category here. Hurricane Harvey in 2017 put roughly 3,000 Friendswood homes under floodwater, by the preliminary estimates reported that September, and the city issued 1,787 permits for repairs that year. Beryl did damage again in 2024. When a house is gutted to the studs, the pile is wet drywall, insulation, flooring and baseboard, and that is the pile the 20-yard exists for. Wet material is not a billing problem, because there is no weight fee for us to add.

City Pickup

What Friendswood Garbage Service Will and Will Not Take

Friendswood residents get garbage pickup twice a week, plus weekly recycling and weekly green waste, through Waste Connections, the company the city contracts with. The rules are on the city's garbage and recycling page. Carts go to the curb by 7 a.m. Grass, leaves, and trimmings go in clear bags that weigh no more than 35 pounds. Limbs and branches are cut, tied, and bundled, at most 50 pounds a bundle, 18 inches across, and 5 feet long.

That is a good system for a week of yard work. It is not built for a torn-out fence or a remodel. The page we read does not describe a pickup for furniture, flooring, drywall, or other construction debris, so before you plan around the curb for any of it, call Waste Connections at 281-331-0810, the number the city prints, and ask what they take and when. When the pile is bigger than a bundle, longer than 5 feet, or not yard waste at all, that is where the can starts to make sense.

Paint, pool chemicals, batteries, and motor oil belong in neither place. The city sends Friendswood residents to the City of Pearland's Stella Roberts Recycling facility on Magnolia for household hazardous waste, for a small fee by the pound. Our own list of what stays out of the can is on the rental process page.

Roll-Off FAQ

Friendswood Dumpster Questions

Do I need a permit to put a dumpster in Friendswood?

On your own driveway we have not found a city permit for it, and the driveway is where we set cans. The code of ordinances has no dumpster section that we could find. It does require a permit from the city manager when building work blocks a paved street or alley (Section 70-20), and it bars rubbish on the street in front of a home (Section 26-106). So a street drop is a question for the city. Call Community Development at 281-996-3201 before you book, and we will work around what they tell you.

Will the city's garbage pickup take a remodel or a storm cleanout?

Probably not the way you need. The city's garbage page covers carts twice a week, weekly recycling, and weekly green waste in clear bags of 35 pounds or less or bundled limbs of 50 pounds or less, 18 inches across and 5 feet long. It does not describe a pickup for furniture, flooring, drywall, or other construction debris. Call Waste Connections at 281-331-0810 and ask what they take. When the pile outgrows that, the can is for it.

How long can a dumpster sit in my driveway if my subdivision has an HOA?

That depends on your deed restrictions or association rules, and we cannot read them for you. Many Friendswood subdivisions have an association and a few small ones do not. Find the rule, then tell us the window. The $399.99 weekend special on the 14-yard is dropped Saturday morning and gone by Sunday night, and a one-day rental is $379.99 on the 14-yard or $499.99 on the 20-yard.

How much driveway does the can need?

Both cans are 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide. The 20-yard has taller sides, around 6 feet, so it takes no more ground than the 14-yard. Leave a clear path from the street, move the cars, and look for low limbs or wires over the drive. A photo of the approach texted to 832-425-7749 gets you a straight answer on whether it fits.

Which size should I rent for a roof tear-off or a storm gut-out?

Start with how much you are throwing out, not how much room you have, since both cans fit the same space. One room or a garage is a 14-yard job: $379.99 for a day, $419.99 for 3 days, or $549.99 for a week. A whole roof, or a house stripped to the studs, usually points to the 20-yard: $499.99 for a day, $549.99 for 3 days, or $649.99 for a week. Shingles and wet material get heavy, so call before you load and we will tell you where to stop filling.
