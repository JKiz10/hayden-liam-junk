The Lay of It

A Town That Grew Up Under Its Own Canopy

Turn off NASA Parkway into Taylor Lake Village and the light changes. The road narrows, the canopy closes over the top of it, and suddenly you are somewhere much quieter than the highway you were on a moment ago.

Those trees are why people live here and they are also why we get called. Mature hardwoods drop a lot of material. Every hard blow leaves limbs across the yard, and the ones that do not come down that day come down the next season instead. It piles up along a fence line because hauling it yourself means either a trailer you do not own or a dozen trips in a pickup.

Add water to that. Taylor Lake reaches into this town, and a lot of these lots run back to a bulkhead, a slip, or a dock. Water frontage generates its own category of clutter, and it never looks like household junk. It looks like coolers, cushions, cracked fenders, a spool of line, and a stack of boards that used to be a walkway.

Yard & Storm Debris

Limbs Come Down Whether You Planned On It or Not

The seasonal version is manageable. A blow comes through, you spend a Saturday dragging what you can reach into the corner of the yard, and there it sits looking worse every week. The storm version is not manageable at all. It fills the drive, blocks the gate, and there is no version of a pickup truck that solves it.

Either way we load it. Cut limbs, branches, trunk sections you were able to break down, pulled shrubs, palm fronds, bagged clippings, and the general green mess a shaded lot generates in a year. If you can drag it into one pile before we get there, do. One pile beats five scattered ones by a wide margin, and a faster load is an easier day for everybody.

On the Water

Dock Boards, Boat Gear, and the Space Under the Deck

Waterfront property collects things that never make it back into the house. Old boards pulled off a dock during a repair. A bench seat that came out of a boat and got leaned against the fence. Life jackets nobody would trust anymore, a cracked cooler, hose, line, buckets, and a trolling motor that has not run in years. The space under a deck ends up holding all of it because the space under a deck holds anything.

We clear it out. What we need from you is that the material is out of the water and stacked somewhere we can reach on foot. We are haulers, not divers, and we do not pull pilings or work off a boat. Treated marine lumber soaked in chemicals is one of the few things we have to turn down, so flag anything questionable when you call.

What Rides Out With Us

Taylor Lake Village Questions

Things People Here Check On First

There are limbs down all over the back yard. Do I have to cut them up first?

Cut anything longer than you could drag yourself, and leave the rest to us. We are not running saws, so a trunk section that takes four people to roll is a problem. Limbs, branches, palm fronds, pulled shrubs, and bagged debris are all fine as they are. One favor that saves real time: rake it into a single pile instead of five. One pile loads in a fraction of the time.

Can you haul old dock boards, bulkhead lumber, and boat gear?

Yes, once it is out of the water and stacked somewhere we can reach on foot. Deck and dock boards, rotted rails, old bumpers and fenders, cushions, coolers, tarps, and the bench seats that came out of a boat years ago all load fine. We do not go in the water, we do not pull pilings, and we cannot take treated marine debris soaked in chemicals, so ask us if you are unsure about a piece.

The pile is at the very back of a deep lot. Does that change anything?

It changes how long we are there, not whether we will do it. Long carries through side yards and back gates are routine around here because that is how these lots are built. Tell us roughly how far it is from the street and how wide the gate opens. If a wheelbarrow fits, we are in good shape.

Will you take down our old shed or the rotted deck?

No, and we would rather say so plainly. We are insured to haul, not to take structures apart. What we will do is empty the shed down to the bare floor before your contractor starts, and come back and clear every board and every screw once the tear-out is done. Plenty of Taylor Lake Village jobs run in exactly those two halves.

Do you come out after a storm rolls through?

That is a busy stretch for us, and yes. With this much canopy overhead the debris here is heavier and messier than it is a few streets inland. We usually work storm cleanup in passes, first the big stuff blocking the drive, then the rest once you have had a chance to sort through it. Call or text 832-425-7749 and we will tell you honestly where you land on the schedule.

How do you charge, by the load or by the hour?

Neither one gets quoted blind. We look at what is actually there and give you a number for free, with nothing attached to it. Brush is bulky and light, an old workbench and a boat trailer full of gear is neither, so a single rate would be wrong half the time. Text a photo of the pile to 832-425-7749 and you can usually skip the site visit.
