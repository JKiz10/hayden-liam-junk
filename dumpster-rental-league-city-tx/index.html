Before You Book

Driveway First, Street Only With the City's Say-So

On almost every League City call we say the same thing: use the driveway. It keeps the can out of the roadway and the curb drainage, and it keeps the list of people who have to approve it short.

The street is where the paperwork starts. League City's code, Sec. 98-5, bars anyone from placing materials, boxes, or other objects in or on a street, alley, or sidewalk without the written permission of the building official. A roll-off is an object. We searched the code and the city's building fee schedule and found no dumpster permit, fee, or time limit, so we cannot say what the city will ask of you. If the driveway really will not work, ask the city before you book. The city's page on applying for a permit lists the main line, 281-554-1000, and the online permit portal. Tell us what the city says and we will set the delivery to match.

Then there are associations. South Shore Harbour, Tuscan Lakes, and Magnolia Creek all have them, and deed restrictions can say more about a driveway than the city does. We found no published dumpster rule for any of the three and will not guess at yours. Ask how many days your association allows and whether it wants approval in writing. If your window is short, book the one-day or the weekend rental.

Look up, too. Live oaks line Main Street (the Butler Oaks were planted in 1907), and some older blocks have limbs hanging over the drive. A can coming off a trailer needs headroom, so snap a picture from the curb before your date.

City Pickup

What the Curb Pickup Will and Will Not Take

AmeriWaste is the city's trash contractor, and the city splits town into three pickup zones. Heavy trash and yard waste ride along on your regular days under the city's heavy trash rules:

  • Up to two heavy items per collection day, set at least four feet from anything else.
  • Each one has to be something two people can lift onto a truck.
  • Set it out by 7 a.m. on collection day, but not before 6 p.m. the night prior.
  • Furniture, washers, dryers, and stoves are fine. A fridge or freezer goes only after a pro has drained the Freon and tagged it.
  • Yard waste tops out at six bags or bundles per pickup, each under 50 pounds. Tree trunks and root balls stay behind.

Construction waste is the part that matters to a roll-off. The city's garbage and recycling pages say AmeriWaste does not pick it up with regular trash and that construction debris is the resident's responsibility. The city's own list of debris the contractor will not take includes shingles and roofing, sheetrock, tile, carpet, cabinets and countertops, plywood and loose lumber, concrete, and brick. Read that list as a job list: a roof, a bathroom, a floor, a kitchen, a deck.

Residents get two small outs. Once a month you can take a load no taller than a pickup bed (no trailers) to AmeriWaste's Highway 6 facility, with your driver's license and a paid water bill, after calling to confirm the items. AmeriWaste also does a small-load pickup for a fee if the debris is bagged. That suits a closet of drywall, not a roof.

Waiting at the curb has a cost too. The city's list of common code violations includes piles of trash, rubbish, and other debris visible to the public. And from time to time the city runs a sign-up curbside round for TVs, electronics, microwaves, and appliances, which is separate from heavy trash day.

What Fills a Can Here

League City Jobs and the Can That Fits

The Census Bureau's 2024 survey puts the median League City home at a 2004 build. About a third of League City's homes went up between 2000 and 2009, roughly 80 percent are detached single-family homes, and about three in four are owner-occupied. Many of those houses are now old enough that the first roof, the original carpet, and the builder-grade fence are coming out.

The older ends of town load differently. Around Main Street, the League City Historical Society's walking tour lists houses from 1886 into the early 1900s, and a remodel in a house that age tends to turn up plaster, lath, and layers of old flooring. South Shore Harbour began in the early 1980s, so its original kitchens, baths, carpet, and decks are at the age where they come out.

Here is how we would match the job to the can:

  • Garage, attic, or one bathroom: the 14-yard. The $399.99 weekend rate gives you Saturday and Sunday to load.
  • Roof replacement: the 14-yard for a modest roof, the 20-yard for a big one or one with more than one layer. Re-roofing over 100 square feet needs a permit under the city's building permit list, so ask your roofer how many days the tear-off takes and book to match.
  • Fence, deck, or shed tear-out: the 14-yard on the weekend rate. Panels and boards are bulky, so load them flat.
  • Kitchen gut, whole-house flooring, or a move-out: the 20-yard for 3 days at $549.99, so you only dump once.
  • Estate or whole-house clear: the 20-yard on the 7-day rental, $649.99.

None of that carries a weight fee, so heavy loads like tile or wet drywall do not change the price. Fill it to the rim, level, with nothing heaped above it.

After the Weather

Storm Cleanup and Gut-Outs

Storm work is part of life here. In October 2017 the city told the Galveston County Daily News that Hurricane Harvey flooded about 7,700 League City homes, around 23 percent of all residences in League City, and that roughly 1,450 of them took at least 18 inches inside. Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 was a different kind of mess. Mayor Nick Long described it as mostly vegetation down, trees and fences, with no serious flooding.

Those two storms show what goes in a can. A flood leaves wet drywall, insulation, flooring, and baseboard that has to come out fast. Wind leaves fence panels, shingles, and limbs. A roll-off swallows every bit of it. The city's yard waste pickup also skips tree trunks and root balls of any size, which is one more reason a big tree job often wants a can. Tell us what the pile is made of when you call.

League City Roll-Off FAQ

Questions League City Customers Ask

Can I put a dumpster in the street in League City?

Ask the city first. League City's code, Sec. 98-5, says nobody may place materials, boxes, or other objects in or on a street, alley, or sidewalk without the written permission of the building official. We found no separate dumpster permit, fee, or time limit in the code or in the city's building fee schedule, so we cannot tell you what the city will say. The driveway is the simple answer. If you truly need the street, get the city's answer first and relay it to us, and we will set the delivery to suit.

How long can a roll-off sit in a South Shore Harbour or Tuscan Lakes driveway?

That is up to your community association and your deed restrictions. We did not find a published dumpster rule for either neighborhood, so ask yours how many days it allows and whether it wants approval in writing. Then match the rental to the answer. On the 14-yard, $379.99 buys one day and $399.99 buys the weekend. Extra days run $50 when the can is free.

Will League City's heavy trash pickup take my remodel debris?

No. The city's contractor, AmeriWaste, takes up to two heavy items per collection day, such as furniture, washers, dryers, and stoves, and the city says construction waste is the resident's responsibility. Its list of debris the contractor will not take includes shingles, sheetrock, tile, carpet, cabinets, plywood, and concrete. That is the material a roll-off is for.

What size do I need to replace a roof or tear out a fence in League City?

A modest single-layer roof usually fits the 14-yard, and a big roof or one with more than one layer is 20-yard work. Fence panels and deck boards are bulky, so the 14-yard on the $399.99 weekend rate is a common fit for a fence run. Re-roofing more than 100 square feet needs a city permit, so ask your roofer how many days the tear-off will take and rent for that long. The 20-yard starts at $499.99 for a day.

Can I put wet drywall and flooring from storm damage in the can?

Yes. Wet drywall, insulation, flooring, fence panels, shingles, and cut limbs all go in a roll-off, and wet material carries no extra charge, so load to the rim and no higher. Keep out paint, chemicals, tires, batteries, and Freon appliances. Unsure about something? Call before you toss it in.

Can you set a can on a tight driveway under the oaks near Main Street?

Often, but headroom matters more than width. A can needs a flat spot roughly 14 feet long with nothing low overhead while it slides off the trailer. Send a picture of the driveway from the curb with any low limbs in the frame. If it will not clear, we will tell you before delivery day. Boards go under the can either way.
