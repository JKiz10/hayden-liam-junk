Before You Book

Where the Can Will Sit

Placement decides whether delivery day goes smoothly, so settle it first. A roll-off needs a flat, clear spot about 14 feet long and 7 feet wide, plus a straight run for the truck to line it up. In Webster that spot is usually one of three places.

  • A home driveway. Text us a photo from the street so we can look at the approach, the slope of the apron, and any limbs or wires overhead.
  • A condo or apartment lot. The property decides, not you. Get the manager's okay first and ask where a container is allowed, so it does not end up across a fire lane or in front of the building's own trash enclosure.
  • An office or retail service area. Pick the loading side, away from customer stalls, with room for the truck to line up straight.

What about the street? We read Webster's code of ordinances and did not find a section on where a rental dumpster may sit. The city does have a right-of-way use permit, but its application is written for construction and utility work, with insurance and a bond, not for a rental can. The drainage page also asks residents not to leave debris next to the curb, the driveway, or in the street, because it clogs the storm drains.

So the plan that works is the driveway or a private lot. If the street is the only place it fits, call Public Works and Engineering at 281.316.3700 before you book and ask. Their Public Works page is the place to start, and the stormwater page has the drainage rule. If the answer is no, we will help you find a spot on the property.

Webster is small and flat, and the city says so itself. City Trash or a Can?

What Webster's Pickup Takes, and Where It Stops

Start with what the city already does, because if your load fits, you do not need us. Webster's code says a trash can and everything in it should weigh 50 pounds or less, and anything that will not fit in a receptacle has to be broken into pieces under 50 pounds. Limbs, shrubs, and hedge cuttings have to be bundled no longer than five feet and no heavier than 50 pounds.

Heavy material is a different category. The code lists brick, broken concrete, rock, lumber, dirt, plaster, sand, gravel, and dead trees, and puts the cost of getting rid of them on the owner, with any fee set by the city council and kept on file with the city secretary. Lumber is on that list by name, and a fence, a deck, or a remodel produces plenty of it. That is where a can makes sense.

Two more rules explain why a can on your own property helps. The code bars junk or rubbish in a street, an alley, or the strip between the curb and the sidewalk, and it says debris cannot go to the curb before 5 p.m. the day before collection. Rubble left to pile up on a property is the owner's job to clear, with ten days' notice before a violation is charged.

The city also runs a spring clean-up week. In 2026 it ran March 23 to 27, with free curbside pickup for residents of trash, furniture, appliances, and tree limbs, plus a drop-off at the Public Works Service Center on Magnolia Avenue with proof of Webster residency. It did not take chemicals, paint cans, vehicle parts, or tires. The city's notice has the details. Beyond what the code describes and that one week, we did not find a bulk-pickup schedule on the city's pages, so ask Public Works what your address gets.

Paint, oil, batteries, and tires stay out of our cans, and the city's trash contractor will not take them either. Harris County takes household hazardous waste by appointment from residents outside the City of Houston. See its county page.
