Before You Book

Driveway, Curb Strip, or Street: What the City Code Says

Settle where the can will sit before you worry about size. We put almost all of ours on driveways, and Missouri City has plenty of them: Census Bureau American Community Survey five-year figures for 2020 through 2024 count about 88 percent of the city's housing units as single-family detached houses, and about 82 percent of occupied homes as owner-occupied. Walk out with a tape measure before you pick a date, because a can takes 14 feet by about 7 feet of flat ground.

The street is the part that catches people out, so we went to the source. Chapter 46 of the city's code of ordinances, in section 46-22, says nobody may leave equipment or materials sitting in the public right-of-way unless the director of public works has approved it in writing, and the city asks for a bond. Streets and sidewalks fall under the code's definition of right-of-way. The city's right-of-way page adds that it generally runs from the property line to the back of the curb or the pavement edge, which pulls in the grass strip as well as the asphalt.

Neither word, dumpster or roll-off, appears in that section, so there is no rule written for our cans specifically. We read it conservatively: a can on the pavement, the curb strip, or a sidewalk needs the city's written okay, and we will not leave one there on a guess. Plan on the driveway. If your lot has none that works, call Public Works, Streets and Traffic, at 281-403-8577 before booking. That number comes from the city's streets page. The public infrastructure permits page covers right-of-way work too, though it is silent on dumpsters.

Before the truck arrives, do one walk-through of the drive: cars moved, then eyes up. A low branch or a drooping cable over the pad can stop a delivery, so a quick photo of the approach saves us both a wasted trip.

Association Rules

Sienna, Other Subdivisions, and the Clock on Your Driveway

Many Missouri City subdivisions have recorded deed restrictions, and Sienna, the big master-planned community, has its own residential association. Documents like those can limit how long a container may sit in view of the street. Their terms change from one section to the next, and nobody at H&L has read yours, so go through them yourself before booking.

Most limits can be met with our shortest options: a single day at $379.99, or the $399.99 weekend. Tell us the deadline and we will schedule the delivery and the pickup around it. If your section allows no container in view at all, skip the can. Our Missouri City junk removal crew can haul the pile away in one visit, which leaves nothing sitting outside.

City Pickup

What the City's Bulk Pickup Takes, and What It Refuses

Missouri City runs its own garbage program, and the city's solid waste page names GFL Environmental as the contractor. Trash goes twice a week. Recycling, yard waste, and bulk waste each go once a week, all on one day, and that day is Tuesday in some neighborhoods and Thursday in others. Hunters Glen, Quail Green, and Bradford Village are on the Tuesday list. Quail Valley East, Plantation Trails, and Oyster Creek are on the Thursday list. Put everything out by 7 a.m.

The city code, in chapter 78, describes bulky waste as household things that will not fit in a poly cart: furniture, large appliances, swing sets, bicycles without tires, and scrap metal. A refrigerator needs a technician's certificate that the refrigerant is out. Limbs and brush must be bound together, 4 feet long at most and no more than 40 pounds a bundle, while grass and leaves go in sturdy compostable bags. Nothing goes to the curb before 6 p.m. the evening before.

The same chapter bars what it calls excluded waste, a term that covers any waste from construction, demolition, remodeling, or repair. By that definition a gutted bathroom, a stack of old cabinets, or the drywall from a repair does not belong at the curb. The city page prints no per-pickup limit and no item-by-item list, so take any question about a specific item to GFL's Missouri City line, 281-403-5800. Remodel debris, or any pile bigger than one week's pickup can carry, is where a can pays off.

Roll-Off FAQ

Missouri City Dumpster Questions

What does a roll-off cost in Missouri City?

Fourteen-yard rates: 1 day $379.99, weekend $399.99, 3 days $419.99, 7 days $549.99. Twenty-yard rates: 1 day $499.99, 3 days $549.99, 7 days $649.99, with no weekend option. Each price includes delivery and pickup, and we are fully insured. A day added to a rental is $50 if the can is free. To hold a date, call or text 832-425-7749.

Is a roll-off allowed in the street in Missouri City?

Not without the city's written approval, as far as we can tell. Section 46-22 of the city code says equipment or materials may not sit in the right-of-way unless the director of public works approves it in writing first, with a bond. Right-of-way covers more than the pavement: the sidewalk and the grass strip count too. The section never says dumpster, so we keep our cans on driveways. No driveway? Call Public Works, Streets and Traffic, at 281-403-8577 before you book.

Can I put remodel debris out for the city's bulk pickup?

Usually not. In the city code, bulk waste means things like furniture, large appliances, and scrap metal, while waste from construction, demolition, remodeling, or repair is on the refused list. Brush has to be bound into bundles, 4 feet long at most and 40 pounds at most. GFL, the city's contractor, answers questions at 281-403-5800. A remodel pile is the job a can is built for.

How long can a dumpster stay in a Sienna driveway?

Your association sets that limit, and it varies by section, so check your own paperwork first. If the limit is tight, book the one-day rental ($379.99) or the weekend ($399.99, Saturday morning through Sunday night) and give us your deadline when you call.

Is there a weight limit on the dumpsters?

Neither can carries a weight charge. Fill to the rim and stop there, so the load can be strapped and hauled legally. Heavy material such as concrete, tile, or dirt needs a quick call to us first, so we can explain how to load it.

Do you rent anything bigger than the 14-yard?

Yes. The 20-yard is $499.99 for 1 day, $549.99 for 3 days, or $649.99 for 7 days, and it has no weekend rate. Its footprint matches the 14-yard, and its walls rise about 6 feet, so you gain room without asking more of your driveway. Delivery, pickup, and insurance are the same as on the smaller can.
