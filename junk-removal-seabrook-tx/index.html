The Town

A Small City That Is Mostly Water

On paper Seabrook is about 21 square miles, and roughly three quarters of that is water. What is left is a bay town of around 13,600 people where Clear Lake runs into Galveston Bay, with Kemah just over the bridge. A lot of the junk comes from living that close to the water.

Salt air and humidity are hard on anything kept in a garage or out back. Decking goes soft, shelving rusts, and cardboard turns to pulp. Our yard is at Able Self Storage in Pearland, about 16 miles from Seabrook in a straight line, well inside the 20-mile circle we cover. City Pickup

What Seabrook's Bulk Pickup Actually Covers

Seabrook contracts its trash and recycling to Waste Management, and State Highway 146 is the dividing line. West of it, the Green Zone gets bulk pickup every other Monday. East of it, the Yellow Zone gets bulk every other Tuesday. Either way the city limits each bulk collection to two items.

Here is the rest, per the city's trash and recycling page, as of October 2026. Rules change, so read the page before you drag anything to the curb.

The City Takes

Household furniture, mattresses, bagged trash, carpet cut into four-foot bundles, and appliances once the refrigerant has been drained and tagged by a certified recycler.

The City Refuses

Concrete, brick, asphalt and roofing. Contractor construction debris. Paint, motor oil, gasoline, batteries, tires, vehicles and parts. Anything over 200 pounds.

Yard Waste

Two cubic yards on bulk day. Limbs no thicker than six inches, cut to four-foot lengths, tied in bundles of 50 pounds or less.

When to Call

Where the Two-Item Rule Runs Out

Two items every other week is fine for a worn-out chair. It is not enough when you are clearing a garage, closing out an estate, or turning a rental before the next tenant, and the city would still refuse the heavy and the messy parts.

Call a crew when there are more than two things, when something weighs over 200 pounds, when it is remodel or flood debris, or when you cannot wait for your zone's next bulk day. We carry from wherever it sits, the garage, a back room, a staircase, or the far end of a yard, and we load it ourselves.

We do not take wet paint, motor oil, gasoline, propane, or chemicals. For those, the city's page points residents to the Harris County household hazardous waste facility on Hahl Road, by appointment only.

Houses and Streets

Narrow Old Blocks, Small Lots, and Docks

This is not one kind of neighborhood. Old Seabrook, the historic district around Main Street east of SH 146, has small blocks and narrow streets by the city's own description, which means a longer carry and a trailer parked with some thought. Older sections like El Jardin, around Louise and Oleander, have homes going back to the 1960s on modest lots. Subdivisions such as Baybrook fill in the rest.

The city zones some sections for small-lot and zero-lot-line homes, plus condos and townhomes, so side yards can be tight. Then there are the bay-front lots along Todville Road, and plenty of properties across town have a private dock. Waterfront jobs are usually more about the carry than the load.

We work off NASA Road 1, SH 146, Red Bluff Road and Todville Road, and down on Waterfront Drive by the fish markets. The city does not enforce deed restrictions, so in a subdivision with an association the HOA decides what can sit out front. If we load from the garage or side yard, nothing waits at the curb either way.

After the Water

Storm Years Leave Piles

The city puts Seabrook in Zip Zone A for evacuations. After Hurricane Ike in September 2008, city officials say the surge reached about 12 feet. Many residents went without power and running water for up to a month. Harvey brought high tides but no surge. Hurricane Nicholas in September 2021 put about 18 inches of water on the Todville Road area, affected 30 homes, and tore away the 1,000-foot Pine Gully fishing pier (Community Impact).

After a storm the pile is the same on every street: soaked drywall, carpet, cabinets, mattresses, and fence panels. The city's bulk rule is two items and its list excludes contractor construction debris, so a gutted room is a private-haul job. If a repair bill reaches half the building's value, the city's floodplain page says the work needs permits and an elevation certificate, so a bad flood can become a full rebuild. Take your photos for the adjuster first, then we can clear it.

We will not pretend we can be there the morning after, because everyone calls at once. We take jobs in the order they come in, so save 832-425-7749 before the season starts.

Things Seabrook Folks Ask Us

When does Seabrook's bulk pickup come, and how much will the city take?

Per the city's page, as of October 2026, State Highway 146 splits the city. West of it you are in the Green Zone, with bulk pickup every other Monday. East of it you are in the Yellow Zone, with bulk every other Tuesday. The limit is two items per pickup and nothing over 200 pounds. If you have more than that, or you cannot wait two weeks, call or text 832-425-7749 and we will come load it.

The city will not take contractor debris. What do I do with remodel or flood gut-out debris?

That is crew work. Drywall, flooring, cabinets, lumber, and soaked furniture from a remodel or a room that took on water go on our trailer, and we do the carrying. We cannot promise the morning after a storm, because everyone calls at once, but we take jobs in the order they come in. Call or text 832-425-7749.

Can you work Old Seabrook's narrow streets and small lots?

Yes. The city describes Old Seabrook as small blocks and narrow streets, and some other sections have small lots and tight side yards. We look at the access before we come out, decide where the trailer sits, and carry by hand. Text a photo of the driveway and the pile and we will tell you what we see.

We have a dock and a bulkhead behind the house. Can you haul the old boards?

Yes. Old decking, dock boards, bulkhead lumber, and the clutter that collects under a waterfront house are normal work in a bay town. We break down what needs breaking down, carry it up the yard, and load it without dragging it across your lawn. Fully insured, and the quote is free.

Do you take the paint, motor oil, or tires that the city refuses?

Not paint, motor oil, gasoline, propane, or chemicals. The city's bulk list refuses paint, oil, and gasoline too. For tires, or a fridge that still has its refrigerant, tell us what you have and we will say yes or no before anyone drives out.
