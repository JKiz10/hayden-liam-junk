No Surprise Bill

We call it the No-Scale Rate. The roll-off companies that charge by weight do it because their landfill bills them by the ton. Ours bills us by the yard, so a full dumpster costs us the same whether it is couch cushions or roof shingles.

Before Anything Else

Put It on Your Lot, Not in the Road

Space is rarely the problem on a Santa Fe property. The question worth five minutes is where the can is allowed to sit. Our answer is the same on every job: on your own driveway, pad, or yard, with the whole can inside your property line.

The city's code is the reason. Chapter 3, Section 25 of the Santa Fe Code of Ordinances declares obstructions of any kind in a public street right-of-way a nuisance and a traffic hazard, and it lets the code enforcement officer have them removed without notice. We read through that code and the city's website and could not find a permit process for leaving a roll-off in the street. That does not prove there is none, so if you believe your job needs the street, call City Hall at (409) 925-6412 before you book and tell us what they say.

Many Santa Fe streets have an open roadside ditch with a culvert under each driveway. That ditch is how the town drains, and the city's code is firm about not blocking it. When you pick the spot, stay off the culvert crossing and keep the can clear of the ditch line.

A Santa Fe mailing address does not always mean you are inside the city limits. The city's burn permit page, for one, sends city residents and county residents to different offices, and outside the limits the county sets the rules for its own roads and ditches. The advice stays the same either way: keep the can on your property and out of the ditch.

The Approach

Gates, Gravel, and Soft Ground

A roll-off rides in on a trailer and slides off the back, so the truck needs a straight shot at the spot and some room behind it. Before your date, text us a photo of the gate or driveway and the width of the opening. Mention overhead lines and low limbs, and tell us whether the last stretch is gravel or dirt.

Soft ground matters more than distance out here. Santa Fe is flood-prone country, and a loaded can is heavy, so a soaked yard after a hard rain is what moves a delivery, not the length of the drive. If your yard is wet, say so when you book and we will talk through a firmer spot or a better day.

Highway 6 runs through town beside the old Santa Fe railroad line, the one the town is named for, and the city's code names Highway 6, FM 1764, and FM 646 south as its truck routes. A truck that has to leave them may take the shortest way to the drop. Our yard is in Pearland, and Santa Fe sits inside the 20-mile circle on the map above.

What Fills It

Barns, Fence Lines, and Brush Piles

Santa Fe was incorporated in 1978 out of Alta Loma and Arcadia, and it has since taken in parts of Algoa. The city's zoning code still includes an agricultural-residential district meant to keep land open. Jobs here run bigger and more spread out than a garage cleanout.

Sheds and outbuildings. A shed comes down in pieces: shingles, siding, plywood, framing, a rotted floor. Take it apart, carry it over, and the can takes all of it. The 14-yard usually handles a shed. A barn, or a lean-to with a full roof, wants the 20-yard.

Fence lines. Wooden fences were one of the storm debris types the city's Street Department collected after Hurricane Beryl in July 2024, and only until the last pass ended in late August. After that, set-outs went unpicked. A run of old pickets is far more volume than it looks standing up.

Storm cleanup. More than 46 inches of rain fell on Santa Fe over four days of Hurricane Harvey in 2017, according to the Galveston County Daily News, and flooded carpet and furniture ended up in piles on lawns around the area. Wet flooring and storm limbs fill a can fast, and a can keeps all of it off the grass.

Notices from the city. The city's trash and brush ordinance lists overgrown weeds, brush, and loosely strewn building debris such as lumber and fencing as unsightly property that can become a nuisance. A notice gives ten calendar days to bring a lot into line. If one lands in your mailbox, a 3-day 14-yard at $419.99 is usually enough time to clear it. The same ordinance requires an adequate on-site container on construction, renovation, and demolition work, and a rented can is one way to meet that.

Fire Rules

Burn Pile or Can?

On a big lot a brush fire is a normal way to handle limbs, and inside the city limits it takes a permit. The Fire Marshal inspects the pile first. The city's burn permit page says only brush and limbs may be burned, and it lists what may not: shingles, furniture, treated lumber of any kind, railroad ties, wire, and household garbage, among others. It also posts whether a burn ban is in effect, so check it before you stack anything.

So split the job. Limbs can go on a permitted fire if you are willing to wait on the inspection and the right weather. The roof, the shed, the fence posts, and the old couch go in the can. Or put the limbs in the can too and skip the fire. Outside the city limits, the same page sends you to Galveston County Pollution Control at (409) 938-2251.

City Pickup

What the City Will and Will Not Haul

Santa Fe's website does not print a bulk-item pickup rule that we could find. For regular trash days and size limits, call City Hall at (409) 925-6412.

What the city does say is on its Hurricane Beryl page. Household trash went out with the regular contract pickup. The Street Department collected limbs, brush, wooden fences, and bagged storm debris in passes, section by section, and never took household trash or appliances. Once a section's last pass was done, anything set out after that day stayed where it was.

The city's code defines bulky waste as household items too big for a garbage container, such as furniture and appliances, but a definition is not a pickup schedule. Ask before you drag a couch to the curb.

A can makes sense when the load is bigger than a curb pile, when it is lumber, fencing, roofing, torn-out flooring, or a shed, or when you do not want to wait on anyone's schedule. A short list stays out of every roll-off, and it is written out in the what can't go in section of our process page. If you are unsure about an item, ask us before it goes in.

Questions From Santa Fe Customers

Can I put a dumpster in the street in Santa Fe?

We set ours on your driveway or yard. The city's code calls obstructions in a street right-of-way a nuisance and a traffic hazard, and we found no published roll-off permit. If you think you need the street, call City Hall at (409) 925-6412 first and tell us what they say. Never set the can across a culvert or in the ditch.

Will the truck reach a barn at the back of my acreage?

Usually, but the gate and the ground decide it. Text us a photo and the width of the opening, and tell us if the last stretch is gravel or dirt. We set the can where the truck can stand on firm ground, which can be near the road with a short carry to the barn.

Can I burn my old lumber and shingles instead of renting a can?

Not inside the city limits. The city allows only brush and limbs to be burned, with a permit and an inspection, and its list of banned items includes shingles, furniture, and treated lumber of any kind. Outside the city, its burn page sends you to Galveston County Pollution Control at (409) 938-2251. Limbs are the only thing worth the wait. The rest goes in the can.

What will Santa Fe's city pickup take?

The city's website does not print a bulk-item rule that we could find, so call City Hall at (409) 925-6412 for current pickup days and limits. The city's storm page says household trash goes out with the regular contract pickup and that its debris passes never took household trash or appliances. A can makes sense when the pile is bigger than a curb set-out or you cannot wait on a pickup.

Which can fits a shed or a barn teardown, and for how long?

A shed usually fits the 14-yard: $379.99 for 1 day, $419.99 for 3 days, or $549.99 for 7 days. A barn, or a shed plus a fence line, wants the 20-yard: $499.99, $549.99, or $649.99 for the same terms. Both fit the same 14 by 7 foot spot on the ground. Acreage work runs long, so the 7-day term is the safer pick. A day added on costs $50 when the can is free.

My Santa Fe address is outside the city limits. Can you still deliver?

Probably. Our service area is a 20-mile circle around our Pearland yard, not a city line, and Santa Fe sits inside it. Text 832-425-7749 with the address and we will confirm. Outside the city the county, not the city, sets the roadside rules, so keep the can on your own property and out of the ditch.
