The 14-Yard Difference

It Fits on Your Driveway. That Is the Whole Point.

Twenty and thirty yard containers are built for commercial lots, tear-downs, and property with room to spare. They are longer and taller than what we run. Back one into a subdivision in Shadow Creek Ranch or Silverlake and you find out quickly that the driveway you have is the driveway the builder poured: garage door to sidewalk, two cars wide, and not one foot longer.

So the big can does one of three things. It does not fit at all. It fits but hangs over the sidewalk, which puts it in the public right-of-way and turns a simple rental into a permit conversation. Or the driver gives up on the concrete and leaves it along the curb, where it eats a lane on a residential street, sits in front of a neighbor's house, and becomes the most visible object in the section.

A 14-yard just goes on the driveway. That is not a small convenience in a Pearland master-planned neighborhood, it is the entire difference between a weekend project nobody comments on and a container that generates a phone call. On your own concrete, the rental is between you and us. Out at the curb, it is between you, the street, and everyone with an opinion about the street.

We lay boards down before it touches your surface, which matters more here than in older parts of the county because so many of these driveways are stamped, brushed, or colored concrete that you would rather not scar. We line it up square so you can load from the open end without walking a hundred laps, and we can usually leave you room to still park a vehicle beside it.

Deed Restrictions

Keeping the HOA Out of It

A lot of Pearland is master-planned, and master-planned means an active association and a set of deed restrictions written by people who care a great deal about what sits in front of your house. Plenty of those documents put a limit on how long a container can stay in view. Book seven days without reading them and you can end up paying to have a can pulled early.

You have two levers, and they stack.

Keep it short. That is exactly what the single day and the weekend term are for. Saturday morning to Sunday night is a window that lands inside most restrictions we have run into, and it is the same $365 either way.

Keep it on your concrete. A container in the driveway reads as a homeowner doing a project. The same container at the curb reads as a container. Nearly every complaint we hear about starts with a can in the street, which is one more reason the 14-yard is the right tool in these neighborhoods.

Read your own section's restrictions before you book. They differ from one section to the next and we are not going to pretend we know yours. Then hand us the window and we will hit it. If your association wants nothing at all sitting outside, skip the container entirely and let our crew carry it out for you with full-service junk removal in Pearland.
