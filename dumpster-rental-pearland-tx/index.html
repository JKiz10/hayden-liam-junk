The Footprint Difference

It Fits on Your Driveway. That Is the Whole Point.

The standard twenty and thirty yard containers you see on commercial lots and tear-downs are made for property with room to spare, and they run longer than anything we put on a trailer. Back one into a subdivision in Shadow Creek Ranch or Silverlake and you learn fast that your driveway is the one the builder poured: garage door to sidewalk, two cars wide, and not a foot longer.

A longer can ends up one of three ways. It will not fit. It fits but hangs across the sidewalk and into the public right-of-way. Or the driver gives up on the concrete and leaves it at the curb, where it takes up a lane of a residential street, sits in front of a neighbor's house, and turns into the most visible thing in the section.

Ours just goes on the driveway. In a master-planned Pearland neighborhood that matters more than it sounds. A can on your own concrete is your business and ours. A can at the curb is also the street's business, the neighbors' business, and sometimes the city's, which the next section gets into.

Before the can touches your surface we slide boards under it. That counts for more on stamped, brushed, or colored concrete you would rather not scar. We square it up so you can load from the open end without walking laps, and when the slab is wide enough there is still room beside it for a car.

City Rules

Street, Sidewalk, and City Pickup

Plan on the driveway. The street is a different matter. Look at the right-of-way chapter of the Pearland code of ordinances. Section 26-5 requires a permit before anyone erects “any other character of obstruction” on or across a public street, alley, or sidewalk, or in the strip between a lot line and the street. Section 26-3 makes it unlawful to block a street gutter or a drainage way, including a drainage easement on private property. Both are written about fences, structures, and drainage, and neither one names a dumpster. That leaves the question open, and we are not going to guess at it for you.

What we will tell you is to keep the can off the street, off the sidewalk, and out of the grass strip between your lot line and the road. If the driveway is too short or shared, call Pearland City Hall at 281-652-1600 and ask about the right-of-way before you book. That number is printed on the city's code compliance page.

Check the city pickup first. Pearland's trash contractor, Frontier Waste Solutions, collects bulk and brush together once a week, on the recycling day. The limit is two cubic yards, about 6 by 3 by 3 feet or 12 to 15 large outdoor bags. It takes small furniture, mattresses, appliances once the freon is removed and the unit is tagged, tied brush, and bagged green waste. It does not take construction and demolition debris, hazardous or medical waste, rocks, dirt, concrete, dimensional lumber, or limbs thicker than 4 inches. The city's Frontier page links the service booklet with the full list. A kitchen gut, a roof, a fence line, or a whole garage lands on the can side of that list.

You have two levers, and they stack.

Keep it short. The one-day and weekend terms are our shortest stays, and the weekend is a flat $399.99 from Saturday morning to Sunday night.

Keep it on your concrete. A can in the driveway looks like a homeowner with a project. A can at the curb looks like a container left on the street, and that is what draws attention. The short 14-yard footprint is what makes the driveway an option.

Pull up your restrictions before booking. They vary by section and we have not seen yours. Then tell us how long the can may stay and we will fit the rental inside it. Can the can go somewhere other than the driveway?

Anywhere on your own property that will hold it. A side pad, a gravel apron, or a slab off the alley all work. Can I load shingles or broken concrete into it?

Yes, with one caution. We charge nothing for weight, so a heavy load will not change your bill. The limit is what we can tow safely. Shingles, concrete, brick, dirt, and rock reach that weight long before the can looks full, so call or text 832-425-7749 first and we will tell you how high to fill it.
