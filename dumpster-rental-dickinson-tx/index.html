No Surprise Bill

We do not own a scale.

Most roll-off companies around Houston quote a low number that quietly carries a weight allowance, usually about 3 tons, then bill $75 to $95 for every ton you go over it. Our landfill charges us by the yard instead of by the ton, so we have nothing to pass along to you. Fill it to the rail and the price does not move. A Dickinson rebuild puts plaster, tile, and old framing in the can, and that is exactly the load that turns into a weight bill somewhere else.

We call it the No-Scale Rate. The roll-off companies that charge by weight do it because their landfill bills them by the ton. Ours bills us by the yard, so a full dumpster costs us the same whether it is couch cushions or roof shingles.

Start Here

Ditch Streets, Curb Streets, and Your Driveway

Settle where the can will stand before you settle anything else. About three homes in four in Dickinson are detached single-family houses, per Census Bureau figures for 2020 through 2024, so most cans go on a driveway. Pace yours off. We need a 14 by 7 foot patch of firm ground, a straight approach for the trailer, and nothing hanging overhead. You do not need to be home. Tell us the spot and we lay boards under the can.

The street is another matter, and Dickinson has nothing in writing about it. We searched the city's ordinances on Municode for dumpster, roll-off, and container, and found no permit, fee, or form for a rented can. The only dumpster rule sits in the fire chapter and is aimed at businesses: at a commercial site, the dumpster stays 5 feet off the building unless the fire marshal signs off in writing. The separate zoning code is one we could not search, and the city's permit page says nothing about dumpsters, so we will not call the street fine. If the driveway will not work, phone Community Development (281-337-2489), the number on that permit page, before you book.

Not every Dickinson street has a curb. The city's drainage criteria send pavement without curb and gutter to roadside open ditches, and another section of the code bars putting anything in a street, alley, or gutter where it blocks the flow of water. A can has no business over a ditch, over a culvert opening, or on a soft shoulder. Loaded, it is heavy, and wet ground gives way. Describe your frontage when you book, or send a photo.

This was berry and farm country before it was a suburb, and some lots are still big. The city's driveway rule asks for four inches of reinforced concrete but exempts lots of an acre or more, so yours may be gravel or an old thin slab. Either works if it is firm.

Shops along Highway 3 and FM 517 have a lot instead of a drive. Leave 5 feet to the building, name it when you book, and clear it with the landlord first.

The Jobs Behind Most Cans

A Town Mostly Built Before 1990

Dickinson has roughly 8,300 homes by the Census Bureau's count, and half of them date from 1986 or earlier. The 1970s are the biggest decade, about 2,200 houses, and roughly 2,750 more went up after 2000, about a third of the city. A house from the 1970s or 1980s is at the age where the roof, the floors, the fence and the kitchen tend to come due at once.

  • Roofs. Shingles weigh more than they look. Text or call first and we will tell you how high to fill.
  • Fences, sheds, and decks. A privacy fence on its way out is awkward to carry and takes more room than you expect.
  • Kitchen, bath and floor tear-outs. A single bathroom fits the 14-yard. Take the 20-yard when a kitchen and its adjoining floors come out together.
  • Move-outs. About 28 percent of households rent, and about one home in six is in a building with two or more units. A tenant change usually leaves something behind to clear.
  • Garages, attics, and storm limbs. Newer subdivisions have real garages, and a big hurricane-season trim outgrows a four-bundle pickup.

Concrete, brick, dirt, and rock need a call first, for the same weight reason. Chemicals, paint, tires, batteries, and electronics stay out. See the rental process page for the full list.

Read Your Own Paperwork

Bay Colony, Bayou Lakes, and the Clock on Your Driveway

The master-planned neighborhoods answer to associations, and Bay Colony has layers of them. The master association's community site lists sub-associations including The Lakes, The Meadows, The Enclave, and Bay Colony Pointe, run by several different management companies, and it says architectural requests go to your sub-association's manager, not to the master association. Bayou Lakes is newer, with Lennar marketing homes there, and its community page shows an HOA line too.

We cannot read your deed restrictions, and the rules on containers and remodel debris change from one section to the next. Look for two answers: does a temporary container need approval, and how many days may it stay? Then tell us the window.

Short windows are what the one-day and weekend rates are built around. The 14-yard weekend special, $399.99, sits on the driveway from Saturday morning to Sunday night. The 20-yard has no weekend rate. If you only need it briefly, book its one-day price of $499.99, against $379.99 for one day in the 14-yard. Storm Rebuilds

Gutting, Raising, and the Flood Years

Harvey is still in the housing stock. The Handbook of Texas records 51 inches of rain on Dickinson in August 2017 and reports that 80 percent of Dickinson homes took damage. City crews hauled about 170,000 cubic yards in three passes and then ended pickup in November 2017. ABC13 reported at the time that debris removal then became the residents' job.

In mapped flood zones, the flood article in the city code puts new construction and any substantial improvement, meaning work that costs half or more of the house's market value, at least 18 inches above base flood elevation. That is how a gut job becomes a lift, and a lift outlasts a weekend. The article also requires a development permit and defines development to include storing equipment or materials. It never mentions dumpsters, so we cannot say a rented can on a flood-zone lot needs one, and we cannot say it does not. Ask Community Development when you call about the permit for the work itself.

Gut work is bulk, not weight. Wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad, and cabinet boxes fill a can long before they get heavy, and nothing is weighed. Take the 20-yard for the full week, $649.99.

The Cart and the Can

What Curbside Pickup Takes, and Where It Stops

Curbside trash in Dickinson runs through the water district, Galveston County WCID No. 1, which bills for it and uses AmeriWaste as its collector. The district's garbage guidelines, updated in January 2024, describe a 96-gallon cart emptied twice a week plus four extra items in any mix: a bundle of limbs, a bag of clippings, a chair. Yard waste tops out at four bags and four bundles. TVs, computers, microwaves, paint, tires, motor oil, and pool chemicals are never picked up. The city's trash page has the contact numbers.

None of these pages says a word about construction. We found nothing on remodel, roofing, or demolition debris. Ask AmeriWaste at 281-585-3200 before any drywall or shingles go out to the curb. If the answer is no, or the pile is past a handful of items, a can makes sense. It takes the whole job in one visit and leaves nothing at the street.

Is a permit required for a dumpster in Dickinson, on the driveway or in the street?

We found no dumpster permit, fee, or form in the city's ordinances or on its permit page, and your own driveway is where nearly all of our cans go. The street is not covered in anything we could read, and the city's separate zoning code would not load for a search, so for street placement phone Community Development (281-337-2489) before you book. On a flood-zone lot, mention that too. The city's flood article counts storing materials as development and does not say whether a rented can qualifies.

Can the can sit next to a roadside ditch, or on a gravel driveway?

Gravel is usually fine when the ground is firm, and we lay boards under the can either way. A ditch is different. The city's code bars putting anything in a street, alley, or gutter where it blocks the flow of water, so a can should never straddle a ditch or cover a culvert opening. Describe your frontage when you book, or send a photo by text to 832-425-7749, and we will say where it should go.

Will the curbside cart pickup take my remodel or roofing debris?

The water district's guidelines describe a 96-gallon cart plus four extra items, such as bundled limbs, bagged yard waste, or a piece of furniture. They do not mention construction, remodel, or roofing debris, so call AmeriWaste at 281-585-3200, the number on the city's trash page, before you stack any of it at the curb. A torn-out kitchen or a roof is a load a can takes in one visit.

Which size fits a gut and rebuild after water damage?

Usually the 20-yard. Wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad, and cabinet boxes fill a can by bulk long before they get heavy, and nothing is weighed. One room is normally a 14-yard job. A whole-house gut can run past three days, so book the 7-day term, $649.99 on the 20-yard, and add days at $50 each if the can is free.

Can I have a dumpster in a Bay Colony driveway?

That is a question for your association, not for us. Bay Colony runs through a master association plus sub-associations, and the master association's site sends architectural requests to your sub-association's management company. Ask whether a temporary container needs approval and for how many days, then tell us the window. The 14-yard weekend special, $399.99, covers one weekend and nothing longer, and a single day is $379.99.
