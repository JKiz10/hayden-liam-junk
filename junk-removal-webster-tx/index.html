Who Calls Us Here

Webster Is Mostly Rentals, and Rentals Turn Over

About 11,000 people live in Webster, but the city’s own comprehensive plan says the daytime population swells past 200,000, because the place is roughly 80 percent commercial. Johnson Space Center is next door, a hospital district sits inside the city, and the Gulf Freeway side is lined with hotels, restaurants, and shopping centers.

The homes are the surprise. The land use chapter counts about 6,500 residences and puts apartments at 79.2 percent of them. Only around 890 are single-family houses. So the typical call here is a manager with a unit to turn or a business with a space to empty.

Our yard is in Pearland, about 10 miles away in a straight line, so Webster is a short run. We can often get here the same or next day when the schedule allows.

City Pickup

What the City Takes, and Where the Gaps Are

Per the city’s page, as of October 2026, Republic Services collects regular residential trash at no cost to residents on Mondays and Thursdays, and commercial customers must use Republic too. That is all on Webster’s trash page.

Bulk gets its own week. The spring Clean Up Week notice set March 23 to 27, 2026, for free residential curbside pickup of trash, furniture, appliances, and tree limbs, plus drop-off at the Public Works Service Center, 855 Magnolia Ave., with proof of Webster residency. No chemicals, paint cans, vehicle parts, tires, or other hazardous materials. The notice lists no size limits, and we saw no fall week posted for 2026.

That leaves gaps. The next week may be months off, it is residential only, and it assumes you can carry a sectional down two flights of stairs yourself. A lease ending or a closing date does not wait on a city calendar. We load from wherever it sits and take it off the property.

Apartments

Garden-Style Means Stairs

The plan describes most Webster apartments as garden-style, a campus of two to four story buildings. They cluster along Egret Bay Boulevard, NASA Parkway, Highway 3, East Medical Center Boulevard, and El Camino Real, and the plan’s table dates several to the 1960s, 70s, and 80s. Older buildings mean tight stairwells and a long walk from the door to the truck.

A turnover is rarely tidy. It is a mattress, a desk, a few chairs, a bike, bags of clothes, and sometimes a washer somebody left. We carry it down, load up, and sweep what we drop.

If you manage several properties, ask about repeat service. See how we work with property managers and tenant cleanouts.

Offices, Hotels & Retail

The Gulf Freeway Side of Town

Along I-45 and Bay Area Boulevard the city zones most frontage as general commercial. The plan counts 32 retail centers, over 150 restaurants, and 20 hotels with more than 1,700 rooms, and says several centers date to the 1970s and 80s and some hotels have aged.

Aging buildings get refreshed, and refreshing means hauling: bed frames and dressers when a hotel floor is redone, shelving and counters when a tenant leaves, waiting room chairs when a medical office near East Medical Center Boulevard moves. We take furniture, fixtures, and equipment, not medical waste, sharps, or chemicals.

The weekly Republic bin is not a one-time clear-out of a whole space. Read about commercial liquidations if a business is closing. We are fully insured and can often work early or late around your hours.

Houses & Storms

Webdale, Green Acres, Edgewater, and the Creek

The houses are the smaller half of the story. The plan names Webdale, Green Acres, and Brad Court as single-family neighborhoods and Edgewater, a 475-acre master-planned community, as the big newer addition. Kobayashi Road is named for Mitsutaro Kobayashi, an early Japanese farmer who grew fruit and vegetables here back when this was all farmland.

Clear Creek runs along the southeast edge of the city and marks the Galveston County line. The plan says the most flood-prone properties sit right on the creek, though other areas can flood too. The mayor’s note in the city’s October 2017 newsletter says Harvey dropped nearly 50 inches of rain on Webster, that some homes took water and many did not, and that the fall Clean Up Week was cancelled because Republic Services was so busy.

A pickup schedule is a plan, not a promise. When water gets into a house, the pile cannot sit in the yard waiting on the city. See our storm debris removal.

Webster FAQ

Things Webster Folks Ask Us

Does Webster pick up bulk trash, or do I need to hire someone?

Some of it, some of the time. Per the city's page, as of October 2026, Republic Services collects regular residential trash for free on Mondays and Thursdays, and the city runs a free curbside Clean Up Week. The spring 2026 week ran March 23 to 27, and we saw no fall week posted. The notice lists no size limits, so call the city's Utility Department at 281-316-4120 first. If you cannot wait, call us at 832-425-7749.

I manage apartments in Webster and have a unit to turn by Friday. Can you do it?

Often, yes. We start the day in Pearland, about 10 miles away in a straight line, so we can frequently reach Webster the same or next day when the schedule allows. Walk-up stairs are normal for us. Text photos of the unit to 832-425-7749 and we will tell you what we have open.

My shop on Bay Area Blvd is closing. The city says businesses use Republic Services, so who clears the space?

Per the city, commercial customers must use Republic Services for regular trash, and that covers the weekly bin. Emptying a whole space of shelving, counters, desks, and fixtures is a one-time job, which is what our crew does. We cannot take medical waste, sharps, or chemicals. We are fully insured and can often work around your business hours.

Can you haul flood-damaged drywall, carpet, and furniture?

Yes, once the wet material is torn out of the house, we load it and haul it away. Webster saw how fast that pile grows after Harvey in 2017. Tell us what is already out when you call, and we will say what we can pull and what needs a contractor first.

The city skips tires, paint, and vehicle parts. Do you take them?

Not the hazardous ones. We cannot take wet paint, chemicals, fuel, motor oil, or propane tanks. Tires, old TVs, and Freon appliances cannot go in a dumpster, but our crew can usually take most of them, so mention them when you call. The city's Public Works page lists household hazardous waste disposal through Harris County.
