City or County

A Manvel Address Is Not Always Inside Manvel

Manvel is in northern Brazoria County, with Pearland to the north, Alvin to the east and Iowa Colony to the west. The name came from a Santa Fe Railroad official, and the city incorporated in 1960 with about 300 people. The 2020 census counted roughly 10,000, up from about 5,200 in 2010. Subdivisions such as Pomona, Meridiana, Rodeo Palms and Sedona Lakes sit alongside acreage, which is why the city's permit list includes ponds, water wells and septic systems.

The catch: a Manvel mailing address does not prove you live inside the city. The garbage franchise, the parking limits and the burn permits below are all city rules, and they stop at the city limits. Past them you are in the unincorporated county, where Brazoria County reports no zoning ordinance and does not maintain state highways, FM roads or city streets.

Your property tax statement is a quick tell, since a city line appears only if the city taxes your lot. Not certain? Send us the address, or ask City Hall at 281-489-0630.

Placement

Your Ground, Not the Street

By default the can goes on your own property: a drive, a gravel pad or firm yard close to the work, so nobody hauls armloads across the whole lot.

The street raises a different question, and in Manvel we found no clear yes. The city's solid waste, traffic and streets chapters say nothing about a roll-off sitting in a street, and its permit list has no dumpster permit. The one right-of-way form it posts is written for utility crews, with boxes for gas, phone, cable, electric, water and sewer. For vehicles on residential streets, the code says none may stay on city street pavement past 48 continuous hours, none may park on a sidewalk, and on a lot with no sidewalk none may sit within five feet of the curb edge. Those lines were written for vehicles, and the code never says whether a container counts.

So we steer you to the driveway. If it will not work at all, call City Hall at 281-489-0630 before booking and ask whether a container may sit in the street on your block, and for how long. We checked the city's solid waste page, its permit applications page and the streets chapter of the code. Highway 6, Highway 288 and FM 1128 are state roads, and a state road's shoulder is no place for a can.

The Ground

Clay, Culverts, and the Day After Rain

Texas A&M AgriLife Extension's gardening guide for Brazoria County calls this part of the Gulf Coast humid, with periodic drought, intense rainfall and mostly clay soil. For a dumpster, that means a spot that was hard on Friday can be a bog after a Saturday storm. Boards under the rails spread a loaded can's weight, but they cannot save ground that has turned to soup, so mention it at booking if your pad holds water.

Rural lots add the culvert. Where a drive crosses a roadside ditch, it usually does so over a pipe, and on county roads Brazoria County asks for a driveway and culvert permit when someone installs or changes one. A loaded can on that pipe or the soft ditch edge risks a crushed culvert and a stuck truck, so we set it on the yard side, past the pipe.

Look up too. Low limbs and overhead wires change where a can can go, and the gate has to clear the truck and trailer, not just the can. On a subdivision lot, measure the driveway: the can is 14 feet long and about 7 feet wide, and the truck needs a straight shot to back in. Brush and Burn Piles

Burn Pile or Can? Manvel's Rules for Brush

Plenty of acreage owners consider burning a brush pile instead of renting a can. Inside the city, the fire marshal's outdoor burn permit rules narrow that down fast. Only plant growth may be burned (trees, brush, leaves, branch trimmings), it must have grown on the same property, and the lot must be at least half an acre. The pile can be no taller than 5 feet, no wider than 3 and no longer than 10, and it must sit 100 feet from any structure or combustible material and 300 feet from neighboring structures. Burning is allowed from an hour after sunrise to an hour before sunset, and not when the wind is under 6 or over 23 miles per hour. Plastics, rubber, used building materials, cross ties, utility poles and furniture are prohibited.

That list explains where the leftovers go. Old fence panels, rotted deck boards, shed lumber, a broken chair and brush hauled in from another lot cannot be burned, and neither can anything on a lot under half an acre. A can has no acreage minimum and does not care where the limbs grew. These are city rules, so outside the limits ask the county, and check Brazoria County's website for a burn ban first.

Curb Pickup

What the City Hauls at the Curb, and When a Can Wins

Inside the city, Waste Connections handles garbage and recycling under an agreement with the city and bills you directly. The city's solid waste page says garbage goes out twice a week in the blue container, recycling goes out Fridays in the green one, and anything outside a container is not picked up.

Bulky and green waste follow their own rules. Per that page, single-family homes can set out one bulky or white-goods item plus up to four bundles of green waste, at the curb by 7 a.m., on Thursday, or Monday east of Highway 288. Bulky means stoves, refrigerators, washers, furniture and the like, and a refrigerator needs a technician's written note that the refrigerant is out. A bundle must be tied and no more than 50 pounds, 8 feet long and 3 cubic yards. Bulky waste does not include construction and demolition waste.

So the curb works for one couch, one washer or a few tied bundles of trimmings. A can works for everything past that: remodel debris, a fence's worth of panels, a barn's worth of stored junk, or a clearing job that makes more than four bundles.
