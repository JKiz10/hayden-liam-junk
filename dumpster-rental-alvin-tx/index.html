Before the Price

Where the Can Goes in Alvin

The first thing to settle in Alvin is the curb, not the price. Inside the city limits, the town's code says nobody may obstruct or encroach on a street, alley, sidewalk, or other public place unless the code or another law specifically allows it. That is Section 20-1 of the Alvin Code of Ordinances. Section 24-37 adds that no vehicle or other article may be left on city property or a public right-of-way where it would block traffic or cut an approaching driver's view of an intersection to less than 200 feet.

We looked for a dumpster permit, a dumpster fee, or a dumpster application in the street, traffic, and solid waste chapters and did not find one. So we will not tell you there is a permit you can buy to park a can at the curb. We set cans on the driveway or on flat ground you own, and the street stays clear.

If you honestly think the job needs the street, call City Hall before you book and get the answer in writing. The city's garbage and recycling page lists the office and its number.

One more thing that is easy to miss: a ZIP code is not a city limit. Some Alvin addresses are inside the city and some are outside it in unincorporated Brazoria County, where the county says it does not regulate land use or have zoning. Out there, your deed restrictions and the county's road rules do the work. Send your address when you ask for a date and we will say whether we can place a can there.

The Ground Itself

Ditches, Culverts, and Soft Ground

Many roads in and around Alvin run beside a ditch, and the driveway crosses it on a culvert. Alvin's own trash rules mention putting carts on the back side of the ditch line when a shoulder is too narrow. Brazoria County's engineering office says the landowner buys the concrete culvert pipe, sized by the county, and recommends a driveway at least 20 feet wide (county FAQ).

A loaded can is heavy, and a culvert is the weak spot in a driveway. We park on solid ground past the pipe, not on it. For the same reason we ask about a septic tank or drain field if you are on acreage, and about overhead wires and low limbs on the approach. After a hard rain the clay here goes soft, so we put wood under the can to spread the load.

What Goes in a Can Here

Four Kinds of Alvin Jobs

Older houses near the middle of town. Alvin grew up along the Santa Fe rail line and incorporated in 1893, and the old core still has a few houses from the early 1900s. A house that age means tear-outs: worn flooring and trim, rotted porch and siding boards, a bathroom taken back to the studs. Mixed lumber and flooring fills a 14-yard fast.

Newer streets on the edges. The census counted 27,098 people in Alvin in 2020, up from 24,236 in 2010, and newer neighborhoods like Laurel Landing keep adding streets. New streets mean move-ins, move-outs, builder leftovers, garage cleanouts, and fence building. Flattened boxes and broken-down furniture are bulky but light, which is where the 20-yard's taller walls help.

Acreage and farm country. Brazoria County is rice and cattle country, and plenty of lots around Alvin are bigger than a suburban yard. Pasture fence, a collapsed shed, a barn lean-to, and brush piles are mostly volume, not weight, and a 20-yard takes a lot of volume.

Wet-weather cleanup. In July 1979, NOAA records 42 inches of rain at Alvin in 24 hours, a United States record. That is not a forecast. It is just that when water gets into a house here, the carpet, baseboards, and bottom of the drywall come out quickly, and a can is how they leave.

Curb Pickup First

What Alvin's Regular Pickup Takes, and What It Leaves

Before you rent anything, know what the curb will do for you. The rules are in Chapter 11 of the city code, and the city's garbage page sends residents to the city's trash contractor.

  • Brush: cut, bundled, and tied, no longer than 4 feet and no more than 50 pounds a bundle, picked up once a week. Loose brush goes on request, with limbs no longer than 10 feet and no thicker than 10 inches.
  • Bulky trash: anything two people can lift and a regular garbage truck can take. Stack it neatly within 10 feet of the curb, no earlier than 7 days before pickup day.
  • Heavy trash: furniture, washers, dryers, refrigerators. Keep it apart from the bulky pile, at least 3 feet from meters, mailboxes, fences, and trees, and never under low wires or limbs. Ask for the pickup. If you do not, the contractor tags the pile and gives you a week.
  • Not taken: tires, vehicle bodies, hazardous materials, freon appliances without an EPA technician's tag, and lumber, shingles, concrete, and other remodeling debris.

So the plain rule is this. A couch, an old washer, and some trimmings can wait for pickup day. Cabinets, a torn-up floor, drywall, shingles, or a fence line are not curb material in Alvin, and that is when a can makes sense. Not sure which side your pile falls on? Text us a photo. Which rules apply also depends on whether your address is inside the city, so send us the address first.
